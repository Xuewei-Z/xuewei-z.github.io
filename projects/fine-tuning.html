The problem

Paid LLM APIs are easy to start with, but they cost money on every call and keep the model outside your control. The question was whether a small open model, trained on the right data, could do the job well enough.

The pipeline

Web crawler → Text cleaning → 410-example SFT dataset → LoRA fine-tuning → LLM-as-a-judge eval → 4-bit GGUF → llama.cpp + RAG → GKE

What I built

  • Data: a custom web crawler and text cleaning, turned into a 410-example distilled SFT dataset.
  • Training: LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-1.5B on a single T4 GPU.
  • Evaluation: an LLM-as-a-judge harness to score groundedness before and after training.
  • Serving: 4-bit quantization, served with llama.cpp behind RAG, deployed to GKE with Docker and CI/CD.

How I measured it

Groundedness rose from 61% to 86%. After quantization the model ran 2.4× faster and was 3.1× smaller, with no meaningful loss in quality on the same evaluation.

Why it matters

RAG supplies the facts and fine-tuning shapes the behavior. Splitting the job this way lets a 1.5B model run on cheap hardware while staying grounded.
